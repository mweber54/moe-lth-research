Implement, audit, seal, and launch a controlled surviving-initial-value
permutation experiment using the existing 32E IMP80 cohort.

Research question:
Does IMP’s recovery advantage depend on the placement of the original initial
weight values within its surviving connectivity, beyond connectivity and the
within-matrix distribution of retained values?

Hypothesis:
Permuting initial values among surviving coordinates harms IMP recovery more
than it harms matched random-mask recovery.

This is a hypothesis, not an established mechanism or novelty claim.

Suggested directories:
scripts/imp_initial_value_placement_v1/
results/imp_initial_value_placement_v1/

Read repository instructions and inspect:

Locate exact paths from manifests. Do not infer compatibility from filenames.

Use all five source seeds:

For each source use:

2. Experimental factors and bounded cohort

Architecture: 32E.
Nominal expert-weight sparsity: 80%.
All experts, routers and shared components train.

Factors:
A. Mask family:
   - original IMP80;
   - random placement with exact per-matrix IMP survivor counts.

B. Surviving initial-value placement:
   - original;
   - independently permuted within each expert projection matrix.

C. Recovery order:
   - existing registered order A;
   - existing registered order B.

Use the exact saved order A/B arrays and canonical batch streams from
imp_recovery_order_stability_v1. No original-order production runs are added.

For each source:
- one IMP/original model;
- two independently generated random masks, each with original values;
- two independently shuffled-value variants of the IMP mask;
- one shuffled-value variant for each of the two random masks.

This is seven model variants per source, each trained under both orders:
5 sources × 7 variants × 2 orders = 70 endpoint jobs.

Ten IMP/original endpoints may be reused from the completed order study,
subject to full compatibility checks.
Therefore the intended production budget is:
- 60 new 5,000-attempt recovery jobs;
- 10 compatible reused controls.

Random masks and value shuffles are nested realizations, not independent
source-seed replication.

Do not add sparsities, architectures, sources, orders, realizations, or discovery
runs. If control reuse fails, report the blocker before adding replacement jobs.

3. Construct immutable masks and value permutations

For each source/layer/expert/projection:
- obtain W0 from the original initialization;
- obtain the original binary IMP mask M;
- validate shapes, names and orientation against the native model.

Random masks:
- sample uniformly without replacement among matrix coordinates;
- preserve the exact survivor count of the corresponding IMP matrix;
- generate separately for fc1 and fc2;
- preserve the resulting per-expert combined budget;
- do not claim row/column-degree preservation.

Use two predetermined independent random-mask generator seeds, 92001 and
92002, with documented source/tensor-specific derivation.

For any mask M, the original-value condition is:
    W_original[M] = W0[M]
    W_original[~M] = 0

For the shuffled-value condition:
- enumerate surviving coordinates in canonical flattened order;
- randomly permute the list W0[M];
- assign that same multiset back to those surviving coordinates;
- leave every masked coordinate zero.

Use predetermined shuffle seeds 93001 and 93002:
- two independent IMP shuffles;
- random-mask realization j uses shuffle realization j.

Generate transformations with isolated CPU generators. They must not consume
or alter production training RNG states.

Do not shuffle:
- values across matrices, experts or layers;
- expert biases;
- router parameters;
- shared parameters.

Do not reinitialize, rescale, sign-flip, sort values by importance, or fit any
transformation to outcomes.

Store actual coordinate/permutation identities and hashes. Seeds alone are
not sufficient provenance.

4. What is and is not controlled

Within each original-versus-shuffled mask comparison, preserve exactly:
- binary mask;
- per-row and per-column survivor counts;
- cross-projection binary connectivity;
- structural dead-unit counts;
- retained-value multiset within each matrix;
- sign counts;
- squared weight magnitude, up to declared accumulation tolerance;
- biases and all router/shared initial tensors.

Shuffling changes:
- which coordinate receives which initial value;
- neuron-level signed/weighted structure;
- initial activations and model function.

These are part of the treatment, not hidden controls.

IMP and random masks may retain different value distributions from W0.
Do not claim that mask families are matched on retained energy or initial
function. The primary comparison is the difference between their respective
within-mask shuffling effects.

This experiment tests coordinate-specific value placement conditional on the
mask and retained-value multiset. It does not cleanly separate sign, magnitude,
weighted paths, biases, or compatibility with the surrounding network.

5. Training protocol

Reproduce the verified 5,000-attempt, two-segment recovery protocol:
- start from original initialization with the assigned mask/values;
- train all surviving expert weights, expert biases, dense routers and shared
  parameters;
- retain native routing, gates, capacity, auxiliary loss and model configuration;
- maintain gradient masking and post-update zeros;
- preserve learning rate, AdamW, clipping, precision and GradScaler behavior;
- reset optimizer/scaler and RNG at 2,500 exactly as the audited native protocol;
- retain complete model state across that boundary;
- consume the correct second-segment batch stream.

Orders A and B are the saved within-segment permutations of complete batches.
Within each source/order, every variant receives identical batches and the
same native training RNG policy.

Hold each mask and value permutation fixed across the two recovery orders.

Record attempted updates, applied updates, AMP skips, clipping and reset
metadata. Do not extend training to compensate for an unfavorable outcome.

9. Primary estimands

Let L(s,f,j,p,o,t) denote CE for:
- source s;
- mask family f;
- realization j;
- value placement p;
- order o;
- step t.

For IMP:
    Delta_IMP(s,o,t)
      = mean_j L(IMP, shuffled_j)
        - L(IMP, original)

For random masks:
    Delta_random(s,o,t)
      = mean_j [
          L(random_j, shuffled_j)
          - L(random_j, original)
        ]

Primary interaction:
    I(s,o,t) = Delta_IMP(s,o,t) - Delta_random(s,o,t)

Primary endpoint: step 5000.
Secondary: pre-reset step 2500.
Initial and intermediate effects are reported separately.

Positive I means shuffling surviving values harms IMP more than it harms
the sampled random controls.

Also report:
    A_original = mean_j L(random_j, original) - L(IMP, original)
    A_shuffled = mean_j L(random_j, shuffled_j)
                 - mean_j L(IMP, shuffled_j)

Verify:
    I = A_original - A_shuffled

For the primary five-source summary, average the interaction equally across
orders A and B within source, then summarize across sources.

Show every order and realization before aggregation. Shared IMP controls
must not be counted as independent observations for each shuffle.

Do not pool jobs as if there were 70 independent source models.

11. Analysis and interpretation

Report:
- initial CE changes caused by shuffling;
- trajectories and final shuffling penalties;
- IMP-versus-random advantage before and after shuffling;
- primary interaction per source and order;
- variability across nested shuffles/masks;
- applied-update and numerical-accounting differences.

Do not adjust away initial CE statistically and claim that doing so isolates
a mechanism. Report initial and final effects side by side.

Possible conclusions:

A. IMP is harmed more than random:
   Evidence that the placement of its retained initial values contributes
   specifically to its recovery advantage under this protocol.

B. IMP advantage persists after shuffling:
   Exact original value placement is not necessary for the observed residual
   advantage in these tested conditions. This does not prove arbitrary values
   work or that binary topology alone explains it.

C. Both families are similarly harmed:
   A general placement/initial-function effect, without evidence of an
   additional IMP-specific dependence.

D. Effects differ by source or order:
   Report conditional compatibility rather than a universal mask property.

E. IMP does not outperform random in this 32E cohort:
   Report that directly. Do not import the E16 result as a guaranteed baseline
   or interpret the experiment as destroying an advantage that was absent.

A positive interaction does not identify which property matters:
sign placement, magnitude placement, weighted paths, initial function and
compatibility with biases/router/shared components remain candidates.

Do not claim novelty, indispensable individual connections, a routed-data
partition encoded in the mask, or an online pruning algorithm.
