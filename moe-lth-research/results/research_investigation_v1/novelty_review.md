Publication dates below are original submissions/venues, not search-engine crawl dates. Abstract-level versus full-method comparison is identified where relevant.

## Assessment of the selected candidate

**New to this investigation:** independent reproduction of gate contrasts from condition losses, checks against 36 trained endpoint summaries, and direct raw-array reproduction of fixed-source coupling effects. These are audit results, not new training discoveries.

**Existing repository empirical observation:** age-conditioned sparse-minus-dense contrasts remain after assignment replay but become very small after adding detached coefficient replay. This is stronger evidence than small embedding rotations because it changes an explicitly controlled training pathway and has matched dense controls.

**Plausibly novel contribution, not yet established:** a reproducible separation of forward gate-value effects, gate-Jacobian effects and their sparsity interaction, while fixing accepted token assignments and evaluating under common and native policies. None of the targeted papers was identified as conducting this exact four-cell intervention. That search result does not prove no earlier paper did so. A broader full-text citation/backward-forward search is required before a novelty claim in a submission.

**Already known or insufficient:** coefficients scaling expert outputs/gradients; the chain rule for router/expert derivatives; orthogonal invariance of Gram/CKA; drift without spectral change; ordinary mask-induced gradient-angle changes; sparse tickets existing at all. A paper centered only on one of those would be a NO-GO.

The present data do not establish forward-value mediation, a general sparse-expert mechanism, or a useful algorithm. The detachment and readout alternatives are live. Novelty confidence is moderate-to-low; the recommendation is conditional on the discriminating experiment, not a claim of paper readiness.

## Additional contemporary coupling comparison

[Lv et al., Coupling Experts and Routers via an Auxiliary Loss](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/79ff18412c1a5816f071a796375abc3d-Abstract-Conference.html), ICLR 2026, explicitly trains expert/router capability alignment with an auxiliary objective. The official abstract was inspected. This further limits novelty of generic expert–router alignment claims; it does not identify the forward-value versus backward-Jacobian effect measured by the proposed factorial recovery study.
