# Split-half gradient opposition: pilot only

This study is incomplete. The `seed17_B_250` pilot passed the CPU indexing and accumulator checks and the GPU repeatability checks. Its two original-label halves reproduce the archived full-run integer sign sums, resolved counts, accepted occurrences and contributing batches. The model and global RNG state checks passed. The sequence-index intersection between halves is empty.

For the primary 0.8 threshold, 158,989 weights were selected in half 1; all were known in half 2. Both original domain directions were retained for all selected weights, and 70.87% remained strongly opposed. These are pilot results, not a 12-cell conclusion. The marginal-sign independence baseline for opposition was approximately 0.5 in each half, calculated from the observed sign frequencies.

The first shuffled-label permutation retained total eligible accepted exposure per expert but did not exactly match domain-specific exposure. All 32 experts fell within the predeclared 20% matching tolerance for occurrences and contributing batches in both domains and halves. At threshold 0.6 this permutation selected no weights, so its retention fraction is undefined. All 20 pilot permutations have completed. At threshold 0.8, every permutation selected zero weights, making conditional retention undefined in all 20. Their unconditional fractions are zero; the observed fraction is 112,682 / 8,388,608 = 0.01343274. All 20 null values are zero; the paired empirical upper-tail comparison with the prespecified plus-one correction is 1/21 = 0.04762. This small pilot control is not a 12-cell conclusion.

The primary protocol was clarified after the pilot and before inspecting the nine gated cells: use the first 180 retained batches in archived replay order, split 90/90. The nine cells with extra batches also receive an all-retained sensitivity split at the midpoint. The index audit verifies zero cross-half sequence overlap for both rules. Full replay is in progress. No full-study inference should be drawn. Reproducible domain-conditioned gradient opposition does not establish weight importance or that pruning is safe.
