# Historical data availability and limits

Source references: 12 seed/architecture trajectories (8/12/16/24E, seeds 7/17/29). Resolved configs, checkpoint payloads, summaries, validation curves and sampled training/routing logs are present. Original and expanded 12E references are compared at reused ages in `12e_reference_provenance.csv`. Original 16E remains primary; its seed-7 repeat is not pooled. Other sparsities, mechanism interventions, unrelated WikiText architecture grids and older 10k-step suites are not substituted for the 85% router-age localization experiment.

Source routing: `record_train_routes=false`, `record_rich_routes=false`; no complete source assignment or active-step counter exists in these runs. Logged batches are step 1 and every 25 steps (101 batches at R100). Every proposed top-k slot in those batches is counted. Acceptance counts for logged batches can be reconstructed by the audited fixed-capacity rule and checked against dropped_fraction. Validation route NPZ files describe validation, not unlogged training; context snippets are selected examples, not counters. Complete source assignment totals, per-expert min/median/SD/CV, active-step distributions and applied-update counts are NA. R0 counters are exactly zero. HISTORICAL ACTIVE-STEP EXPOSURE UNAVAILABLE for source training.

Recovery routing: accepted/proposed per-expert counts and optimizer-step-applied flags are available in `gradient_stats/gradient_stats.jsonl`. The audit checks unique steps 1–2500 and the proposed assignment sum for every included condition. Step-zero diagnostic rows, where present, are excluded. Complete recovery accepted totals and active-attempt/applied-update counts are available; these are not extrapolated from probes. `recovery_expert_exposure.csv` retains per-expert counts, fractions, zero-token attempts, and descriptive per-parameter ratios. `recovery_pair_exposure.csv` compares native sparse versus dense directly.

Gradients: source gradient norms and historical update vectors are unavailable. Recovery group gradients are fp32 norm measurements after unscaling and before clipping; invalid overflow norms are excluded. Per-expert norm diagnostics are sampled (nominal interval 10), so summed sampled norms and nonzero observations are not full cumulative per-expert gradients. `historical_gradient_exposure.csv` labels this distinction. Full expert nonzero-gradient step counts and cumulative update norms remain NA. No new gradient probes were run.

Checkpoints: source checkpoints permit direct displacement, with physical experts pooled over layers and population SD/CV. Dense source R0 validation is generally not evaluated/saved; its value remains NA rather than substituting a recovery evaluation. `dense_training_trajectories.csv` distinguishes sampled train loss (CE plus weighted auxiliary), language loss, raw auxiliary and validation CE. Missing age losses remain NA. Final loss is R100. `expert_parameter_displacement.csv` only contains ages with saved checkpoints; no interpolation. It excludes the router temperature buffer and counts tied embedding parameters once.

Integrity: 24E source file/model-component hashes are freshly computed; every recovery initial-model hash is reconstructed from source R0 plus its age router and optional mask. Final files/model hashes and zeroed sparse weights are checked. This verifies persisted evidence; initial full recovery snapshots and optimizer objects were not saved, so fresh-optimizer historical evidence comes from metadata and the executed code path rather than resurrected objects. File size/mtime checks cover inputs recorded by the main audit; they are not a claim of historical authenticity. Existing dirty working-tree changes are preserved.

Statistics: seeds are the replicates. Per-expert population SD/CV are descriptive. Ticket-gap uncertainty is sample SD over three seeds. Common-age recovery architecture averages use R0/R20/R100, preventing unequal adaptive grids from silently changing comparisons. These descriptive measurements cannot determine what a longer run would do.
