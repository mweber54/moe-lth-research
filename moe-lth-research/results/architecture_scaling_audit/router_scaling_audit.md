# Router, capacity and initialization scaling

For each layer, `aux = E * sum_e(f_e.detach() * pbar_e)`, where f is the fraction of proposed top-k slots assigned to each expert and pbar is mean full-softmax probability. The transformer averages this quantity over four layers; training multiplies it by 0.1. Balanced routing gives aux=1 independently of E, so its balanced objective contribution is 0.1. No missing or double E normalization was found. Away from balance, the value and gradients depend on the load/probability distribution; this normalization does not equalize router gradient norms or difficulty.

Router projection shape is E by 256 per layer (8192/12288/16384/24576 total router parameters for 8/12/16/24E). Same AdamW learning rate 0.0003 as other parameters. Selected top-1 gates retain their full-softmax probability, rather than being renormalized to 1. Thus increasing E changes gate amplitudes and expert gradient weighting even without a capacity bug. Approximately uniform gates have amplitude 1/E; trained gates need not be uniform. This is shared architecture semantics, not a 24E-only coding error. Historical recovery group router-gradient distributions are in `historical_gradient_exposure.csv`; source gradients were not logged.

Capacity is `max(1, ceil(1.25 * N * k / E))`, separately per layer and batch. At N=16384, k=1, capacities are 2560, 1707, 1280 and 854. Overloaded experts keep assignments with largest selected router probabilities. Rejected contributions remain zero in the MoE residual branch; token loss and attention still operate. Capacity does not spill excess tokens to other experts. Per-expert accepted COUNT can therefore be reconstructed exactly as min(proposed_count, capacity), even without token identities. This audit cross-checks this against the logged layer drop fraction. Top-1 and all four layers are explicit; total routed occurrences across layers must be divided by 4E to obtain exposure for one physical expert.

Default torch Linear initialization has the same fan-in scale for corresponding expert matrices (256 then 1024) and router rows (256), independent of E. Embeddings are constructed before blocks; each block constructs attention, router then experts. More router rows consume more RNG before the first expert; more experts consume more RNG before the next block. Thus same seed does NOT mean identical expert or later shared initialization. Initial embedding draws and first-block pre-router attention can coincide, but whole shared/router/expert states are not paired identities. LayerNorm constants coincide. LM-head construction consumes RNG before its weight is tied to the token embedding. Data permutation uses a separate seeded generator; with data_seed null it uses the experiment seed. `initialization_hashes.csv` documents whole-component identities. No extra E-dependent initialization rescaling was found.

With optimizer.zero_grad(set_to_none=True), an expert receiving no accepted tokens is skipped and need not get a gradient/update. An active forward on an fp16 overflow attempt is not an applied update. Even active applied steps do not establish nonzero gradients on every parameter; AdamW momentum and weight decay further distinguish assignments, gradients and displacement. Recovery counters separate these quantities; complete source active-step history is unavailable.
